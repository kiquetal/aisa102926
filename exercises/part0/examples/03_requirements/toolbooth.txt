This is a good requirements-analysis exercise because the description gives us a reasonable understanding of the system's intended behavior, but leaves several important questions unanswered. Since the system will operate across Texas for 20 years, some of the most important missing details concern correctness, reliability, regulatory compliance, security, scalability, and long-term maintainability.

I would examine the requirements from the perspectives of business rules, functional requirements, quality attributes, external integrations, and operational constraints.

## 1. Business rules that need clarification

These are questions that could change the system's fundamental behavior.

1. How is the toll amount determined?

- Is the charge based only on the toll station, or also on the vehicle type, axle count, time of day, direction, or distance traveled?
- Are there different rates for cars, trucks, motorcycles, and commercial vehicles?
- When a rate changes, does the new rate apply immediately, at a scheduled time, or on a particular date?
- Must the system preserve historical rates so that an old charge can be explained or audited?

2\. What constitutes a toll event?

- Does every camera detection create a charge?
- What happens if the camera reads the same plate multiple times?
- What if a vehicle drives through a station without a valid plate reading?
- How are temporary plates, out-of-state plates, motorcycles, and unreadable plates handled?
- Does the system need to identify vehicle class or occupancy?

3\. What happens when a payment fails?

- Does the system retry automatically?
- Is there a retry limit or a retry schedule?
- Can users update their payment details and retry?
- Are late fees, administrative fees, or penalties assessed?
- What happens if a credit card is declined, expired, or reported stolen?

4\. What does registration mean?

- Is an account associated with one license plate or multiple vehicles?
- Can multiple people own or share a vehicle?
- Can a registered user have multiple payment methods?
- What happens when a vehicle is sold, a plate changes, or ownership changes?
- Does registration take effect immediately, and how are tolls incurred before registration handled?

5\. How are invoices and disputes handled?

- How soon must an invoice be sent after a toll event?
- What happens if the government records are unavailable or return incomplete information?
- What if the registered owner disputes the charge or claims someone else was driving?
- Are there payment deadlines, appeal periods, and escalation procedures?
- How are returned or undeliverable invoices handled?

## 2. Missing functional requirements

The system description identifies several user interactions, but a complete requirements specification should address the following capabilities.

| Area               | Questions to resolve                                                                         |
| ------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Toll processing    | Must toll events be processed in real time, or can they be queued and processed later?       |
| Charge calculation | Are rates versioned, effective-dated, and associated with each toll event?                   |
| Customer accounts  | Can users manage multiple vehicles, household members, and payment methods?                  |
| Billing            | Are multiple tolls consolidated into a single invoice? Are there minimum billing thresholds? |
| Payments           | Are partial payments, refunds, reversals, and payment plans supported?                       |
| Notifications      | Which events trigger email, SMS, postal mail, or other notifications?                        |
| Customer portal    | Can users download receipts, export statements, and view images or evidence of toll events?  |
| Administration     | Which roles can view travel histories, adjust charges, issue refunds, or resolve disputes?   |
| Disputes           | Can a charge be placed on hold while a dispute is investigated?                              |
| Reporting          | What financial, operational, compliance, and traffic reports are required?                   |
| Auditing           | Must every rate change, account update, payment, and administrative action be recorded?      |

A particularly important distinction is between a toll event, a toll charge, an invoice, and a payment. These should not automatically be treated as the same entity.

For example, one toll event may create a charge, that charge may appear on an invoice, and the invoice may be paid through several transactions. The requirements need to define these relationships and the rules for corrections.

## 3. Missing quality attributes

Quality attributes describe how well the system must operate. They are especially important for a statewide system with a 20-year operating horizon.

Performance and latency

- How quickly must a toll event be acknowledged?
- How quickly must a registered user's card be charged or the charge submitted for processing?
- How quickly must an invoice be generated?
- What response time is acceptable for customer and administrator queries?

Availability and resilience

- What uptime is required for toll collection and customer services?
- Can a toll station continue operating if the central system or network is unavailable?
- How are events stored locally and synchronized after connectivity returns?
- What are the recovery time objective (RTO) and recovery point objective (RPO)?

Scalability and capacity

- How many toll stations and lanes will be deployed?
- What are the expected average and peak vehicle volumes?
- How many registered accounts and toll events must be supported?
- How much historical data must remain searchable?

Security and privacy

- How are accounts authenticated and protected against account takeover?
- How is credit card data protected?
- Who can access license plates, addresses, payment records, and travel histories?
- How long may travel and financial records be retained?
- How are access, changes, and administrative actions audited?

Correctness and financial integrity

- How is duplicate charging prevented?
- How are timeouts and ambiguous payment outcomes reconciled?
- How are rates and toll calculations verified?
- How are payments reconciled against the payment processor and financial ledger?
- How can a disputed charge be reconstructed from reliable historical evidence?

Maintainability and evolution

- Can new toll stations, payment providers, notification providers, and government interfaces be added without extensive changes?
- Can toll rules change without deploying the entire system?
- How will hardware and software be upgraded over 20 years?
- What are the requirements for backward compatibility, data migration, and archival access?

These requirements should eventually be made measurable. For example, “the system must be fast” is insufficient; a requirement might state that 99% of toll events must be accepted for processing within a specified time under a defined peak load.

## 5. Important edge cases and ambiguities

The following scenarios should be explicitly addressed before the design begins.

| Scenario                              | Requirement to clarify                                                                      |
| ------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Camera detects the same vehicle twice | Prevent duplicate toll charges without suppressing legitimate consecutive tolls.            |
| Payment request times out             | Determine whether the payment succeeded before retrying, to avoid double charging.          |
| Government records cannot be reached  | Store the toll event and retry lookup under a defined policy.                               |
| Plate number is misread               | Define confidence thresholds, correction procedures, and supporting evidence.               |
| Registered vehicle has a new owner    | Define how account ownership and outstanding charges are handled.                           |
| Toll rate changes at midnight         | Define the authoritative timestamp, time zone, and applicable rate version.                 |
| A payment is reversed                 | Determine the resulting balance, notifications, and administrative workflow.                |
| Customer disputes a toll              | Define evidence requirements, deadlines, review authority, and collection holds.            |
| Station is offline                    | Define local buffering, event ordering, duplicate prevention, and eventual synchronization. |
| Customer account is compromised       | Define account recovery, payment-method protection, and incident response.                  |

One subtle but important issue: a successful camera read does not necessarily prove that the corresponding vehicle was charged correctly. The system needs traceability from the original evidence through plate recognition, rate selection, charge creation, payment processing, and final settlement.

## 6. Legal, regulatory, and contractual requirements

Since the system operates in Texas and handles financial and vehicle-owner information, the requirements should explicitly identify applicable laws, regulations, and contractual obligations.

Areas to investigate include:

- Payment card security: PCI DSS requirements and whether the application should store card tokens rather than raw card details.
- Privacy and records: rules governing collection, access, disclosure, retention, and deletion of vehicle travel histories and personal information.
- Government data access: legal authorization and contractual terms for obtaining motor vehicle records.
- Toll enforcement: applicable toll collection, notice, dispute, late-fee, and enforcement requirements.
- Financial records: retention, reconciliation, auditability, and accounting obligations.
- Accessibility and language: accessibility standards and requirements for English and Spanish interfaces and communications.
- Contractual obligations: service-level agreements, data ownership, incident reporting, continuity requirements, and responsibilities when the 20-year contract ends.

The exact obligations depend on the operator's legal status, applicable Texas law, federal requirements, and the agreements with government agencies and service providers. These should be validated with appropriate legal and compliance experts rather than assumed.

## 7. Operational and organizational requirements

The system also needs requirements for how it will be operated in practice.

- Who monitors toll stations, cameras, queues, payment failures, and integration outages?
- Who is responsible for correcting invalid license plate reads?
- What support hours and response times are required?
- How are production incidents escalated?
- How are toll rates approved, scheduled, and audited?
- How are administrator accounts provisioned and revoked?
- How are software deployments rolled back?
- How are backups tested and disaster recovery exercises performed?
- How are old records archived while remaining available for disputes and audits?
- What happens to the data, software, and operational responsibilities at the end of the 20-year contract?

These requirements help prevent a design that works in a demonstration but cannot be reliably operated statewide.

## 9. How I would use these answers in the architecture exercise

Once stakeholders clarify the missing requirements, I would convert them into measurable quality-attribute scenarios and use those scenarios to evaluate architectural choices.

For example:

- If toll collection must continue during network outages, investigate edge processing, durable local queues, and reliable synchronization.
- If duplicate charges are unacceptable, define idempotent event processing, payment reconciliation, and durable financial records.
- If toll rates change frequently, use effective-dated rate policies with a complete audit trail.
- If the system must remain maintainable for 20 years, establish modular boundaries, versioned integration contracts, data migration strategies, and replaceable infrastructure.
- If travel history is sensitive, establish strict authorization, data minimization, retention rules, and audited access.

The objective at this stage is not to select microservices, a database, a message broker, or any other technology prematurely. It is to establish the business rules and measurable constraints that will justify those choices.

My recommendation: resolve toll calculation and event identity first, payment and failure semantics second, and capacity, availability, security, and retention requirements third. Those decisions will have the greatest influence on the architecture.

Yes, several critical details are missing. I've grouped them by how much they'd hurt if discovered late.

## Highest risk (could break the core design)

**1. Plate reading is unreliable, and there's no error path.**
Camera OCR will misread plates (dirt, glare, weather, damaged or obscured plates). The requirements assume a clean read. Missing: confidence thresholds, manual review queues, what happens with a low-confidence read, and whether a misread can charge the wrong registered customer. That last case is the worst one: charging a real person's card for a car that isn't theirs.

**2. Plate identity is incomplete.**
A license plate number alone is not unique. You need the issuing state or country (out-of-state and Mexican vehicles will cross Texas tolls constantly), plate type (commercial, motorcycle, temporary tags, vanity), and how plate transfers, replacements, and re-registrations are handled. What happens when a registered user sells a car and the new owner keeps the plate, or the reverse?

**3. Payment failure handling is underspecified.**
"Communicate via text or email" leaves open: how many retries and at what intervals, whether the tolls are queued or the charge is reattempted per toll, when the account becomes delinquent, whether it falls back to invoicing, what fees or penalties apply, and whether the user can be blocked or have their registration flagged. Also, charging a card per toll is expensive in processing fees. Most real systems use a **prepaid balance with auto-replenish** or **batched charges**, so this is a core design decision, not a detail.

**4. Government records lookup (DMV/TxDMV) is a hard dependency with no spec.**
You need to know the interface (API, batch file, manual request), cost per lookup, latency, availability, legal permission to use the data, and what happens when no owner is found, the record is out of state, or the record is outdated. Also: is the lookup real-time or batched, and how is the data protected and retained, since it is personal information?

## High risk (compliance and money)

**5. Security and compliance are absent.**
Missing: PCI DSS scope (strongly consider tokenization via a payment processor so you never store card numbers), data privacy law, retention policy for plate images and travel history, encryption, access control, and audit logging. The system also builds a location history of everyone's movements. That is sensitive, and access rules and subpoena or law enforcement request handling need defining.

**6. Authentication and authorization are unspecified.**
How do users log in (MFA, account recovery)? What roles do administrators have? Can any admin see any travel history? Administrators can post payments and resolve disputes, so you need role separation, approval workflows for adjustments, and an audit trail of every admin action.

**7. Disputes process is only a name.**
Missing: who can file one (registered user, invoice recipient), the time window, evidence (the plate image is the obvious evidence, so it must be retained and retrievable), states (open, under review, resolved, appealed), effects on invoices and late fees while pending, and refund mechanics.

**8. Invoicing and collections lifecycle is missing.**
Invoice due dates, reminder cadence, late fees, escalation to collections or registration holds, partial payments, how multiple tolls are batched into one invoice, minimum invoice thresholds (mailing a $1.50 invoice may cost more than it collects), and returned mail handling.

## Medium risk (functional gaps)

**9. Toll rate management.** Rates change over time, so each charge must record the rate in effect at the time of passage, not the current rate. Missing: who changes rates, effective dating, approval, and possible variants such as time of day, vehicle class or axle count, congestion pricing, and discounts or exemptions (emergency vehicles, government vehicles, disabled-veteran programs).

**10. Vehicle classification.** Tolls often depend on axle count or vehicle type. A camera reading only the plate can't tell a car from a 5-axle truck. Do you need additional sensors?

**11. Multiple vehicles and multiple users per account.** Can one account hold several plates? Rental and fleet companies? Can a plate belong to more than one account, and how are shared or business accounts handled?

**12. Timing semantics.** "Immediately charges" needs definition. The charge may not need to be synchronous with the passage. What is the authorization and settlement model? And what is the policy for duplicate reads (the same vehicle seen by two cameras or twice in a short window), and for out-of-order or delayed events from the stations?

**13. Station connectivity and offline behavior.** Texas is large and some stations will lose connectivity. Do they buffer and sync later? How do you guarantee no lost or duplicated events (idempotency)?

**14. Notifications.** Which events notify users (successful charge, failure, invoice, dispute update)? Preferences, opt-out rules, delivery failure handling, and legal notice requirements for mailed invoices.

**15. Refunds, credits, and corrections.** Overcharges, duplicate charges, and misreads all need a reversal process with accounting treatment.

## Non-functional requirements (all missing)

- **Scale:** How many stations and how many transactions per second at peak (holiday traffic, Houston and Dallas rush hours)? Statewide for 20 years implies growth.
- **Availability:** Tolls are lost revenue if the system is down. What is the SLA, and what is the degraded-mode behavior?
- **Auditability and data retention:** Government contracts typically require long retention and the ability to reproduce any charge.
- **Reporting and reconciliation:** Revenue reporting, reconciliation against card processor settlements, reporting to the state, and financial audit support.
- **Longevity:** A 20-year horizon means technology refresh, hardware replacement, changing payment methods (cards are not the only future option), and data migration.
- **Interoperability:** Texas already has multiple toll authorities and tags (TxTag, NTTA, Harris County EZ Tag). Do you need to exchange data or honor reciprocity with them?

## Business and legal questions to resolve with the client

- Who owns the customer relationship and the data when the contract ends?
- Who sets the late fees, penalties, and dispute rules, you or the state, and what do the contract terms require?
- Is there a legal limit on how long after passage you can bill an unregistered owner?
- Is there a cash or non-camera option for people without cards or mailing addresses (unbanked users, privacy-conscious drivers)?
- What is the process for law enforcement and legal data requests?
